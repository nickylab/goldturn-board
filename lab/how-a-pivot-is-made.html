How a Pivot Is Made

Every number in this project rests on one decision: what counts as a turn. That decision is a single parameter. Here is exactly what it does.

1  A high is not a pivot

Price makes highs constantly. A pivot is a high that the market subsequently walked away from far enough that we are willing to call it a top. “Far enough” is the whole question, and k is the answer.

running extreme  E  = highest close since the leg began
confirmation when  close ≤ E − k × ATR
Mirror for a downtrend: confirmation when close ≥ E + k × ATR. ATR is the 14-period average true range at that bar, so the threshold breathes with volatility — k is measured in units of normal daily movement, not dollars.

Two consequences follow immediately, and they are the entire trade-off: a larger k demands more evidence, so it produces fewer pivots — and it takes longer to gather that evidence, so each one is confirmed later.

2  The arithmetic, on a real turn

A genuine peak from the record, stepped bar by bar at k = 2.0. Watch the running extreme ratchet, the trigger price trail beneath it, and the bar where price finally crosses.

3  Turn the dial

Real gold, June 2023 onward. Move k and watch pivots appear and vanish. The hollow marker is where the turn happened; the solid marker is the day it became knowable. The gap between them is the lag you pay.

peak — a down-turn trough — an up-turn hollow = where it happened solid = when it was knowable

5  Why this is the decision, not a detail

k plays two separate roles, and conflating them causes real errors.

As the target it defines which turns we are trying to catch. At k = 2.75 that is 198 turns over 22 years — roughly nine a year, each one large enough to be worth trading.

As a host parameter it sits inside the engine, deciding which candidate signals have a qualifying trend to reverse, supplying two of the twelve scored features, and setting where the protective stop goes. This role has its own optimum and it is not obliged to equal the target.

The measured consequence of that second role: with the current frozen weights the engine peaks at host k = 2.0 and breaks at 2.75 — because those weights were fitted at 1.5 and degrade as the definition moves away from them. Refit the weights and 2.75 becomes viable again. Neither is decisively ahead once the partial 2026 is excluded.
